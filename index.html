Used to put an estimated grade on a card from a photo before pricing it — modeled on the tiers common across major third-party grading services. Treat photo-based estimates as approximate; they are not a substitute for an actual submitted grade.

TierApprox. numericWhat to look for in a photo
Gem Mint10Perfect centering, sharp corners, no visible print lines, edges and surface flawless at normal zoom
Mint9Centering slightly off (up to ~55/45), corners sharp, only extremely minor surface imperfections
Near Mint / Excellent7–8Minor edge wear or light surface scratching visible on close inspection, corners mostly sharp
Excellent / Very Good4–6Noticeable edge whitening, corner rounding, surface scuffing, or minor creasing
Good / Fair2–3Visible creasing, notable corner wear, scratching, or print defects
Poor1Heavy creasing, water damage, writing, major structural damage

How to use this during intake

  1. Get clear, well-lit photos of both the front and back.
  2. Estimate a tier/range from the table above rather than a single fixed number.
  3. Use that range when pulling comparable prices — favor raw/ungraded sold comps unless the photo clearly shows a graded slab with a label.
  4. Always note that it's a photo-based estimate, not a certified grade.
Use the TCG / eBay / SCG links on each collection row to pull live comps at your estimated condition tier, then type the low–high range into that card's entry.
Runs entirely in this browser tab. Scanning a card uses Tesseract.js for on-device OCR (no AI calls, nothing leaves your device for that step); matching the OCR reading against real, released card names calls free public card databases (Scryfall, pokemontcg.io, YGOPRODeck) so you're always confirming against an actual card, never logging raw OCR text. Every logged card has a Price panel with link-outs to TCGplayer, eBay sold listings, StarCityGames, and PriceCharting (which has its own price-history charts), plus this card's own price history inside the app — nothing is auto-fetched; you look the number up yourself and log it, and the card's prior value is kept automatically. Cards priced 60+ days ago are flagged so you know what's worth rechecking; nothing refreshes on its own. Both your Collection and your For-Sale Inventory autosave to this browser's local storage only — nothing you log or import here is written back to the public app source, so this app is safe for anyone to install and use with their own collection. Use Export CSV before closing to keep a backup copy in sync with your own repo.
