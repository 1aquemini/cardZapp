# Condition/grading reference for photo intake

Used to put an estimated grade on a card from a photo before pricing it.
This is a general-purpose scale modeled on the tiers common across major
third-party grading services (e.g. PSA) — treat photo-based estimates as
approximate; they are not a substitute for an actual submitted grade.

| Tier | Approx. numeric | What to look for in a photo |
|---|---|---|
| Gem Mint | 10 | Perfect centering, sharp corners, no visible print lines, edges and surface flawless at normal zoom |
| Mint | 9 | Centering slightly off (up to ~55/45), corners sharp, only extremely minor surface imperfections |
| Near Mint / Excellent | 7–8 | Minor edge wear or light surface scratching visible on close inspection, corners mostly sharp |
| Excellent / Very Good | 4–6 | Noticeable edge whitening, corner rounding, surface scuffing, or minor creasing |
| Good / Fair | 2–3 | Visible creasing, notable corner wear, scratching, or print defects |
| Poor | 1 | Heavy creasing, water damage, writing, major structural damage |

## How to use this during photo intake

1. Ask for (or infer from) clear, well-lit photos of both the front and back.
2. Estimate a tier/range from the table above rather than a single fixed
   number — note it as a range (e.g. "NM–EX, ~7-8") unless the photo quality
   supports more precision.
3. Use that estimated range when pulling comparable prices, since graded and
   raw (ungraded) prices differ substantially — favor "raw"/ungraded sold
   comps unless the physical card has an actual third-party grading slab.
4. If the card is professionally graded (PSA/BGS/CGC/etc.), use the app's
   separate "Professionally graded?" field instead of forcing the slab
   grade into this raw-condition scale — a slab's value is governed by the
   grading company's number, not a photo-based estimate of the raw card.
5. Always disclose that this is a photo-based estimate, not a certified
   grade, since actual grading can differ once a card is physically examined.
